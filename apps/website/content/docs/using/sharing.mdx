---
title: Sharing
description: Who can reach a list, and what they can do with it once they can.
---

A list belongs to whoever made it. Sharing it is one decision, made in one dialog, and
changing it later replaces what was there rather than adding to it.

## Three ways to share

| Sharing | Who sees it |
| --- | --- |
| **Private** | Only the owner. Nobody else knows it exists |
| **Everyone here** | Every member of the instance, including whoever joins later |
| **Named people and groups** | Exactly who you pick |

Alongside that is one switch: whether the people it reaches can **edit** it, or only
read and print it. A read-only share is the right one for a list somebody is shopping
from rather than adding to.

Only the owner can rename a list, share it, or delete it. Sharing a list does not hand
over the list.

## Groups

A group is a shortcut for sharing and nothing else — *Flat*, *Parents*, *Weekend crew*.
Share a list with a group and everybody in it reaches the list; add somebody to the
group later and they reach it too.

It never becomes a second hierarchy. A group has no lists of its own, no settings, and
no meaning beyond who is in it, which is why a group page shows you the lists it reaches
rather than making you go and check each one.

Only an admin makes groups. See [Groups](/docs/admin/groups).

## A public page

A list can be published as a read-only page at a stable link, for somebody with no
account at all — a cleaner, a neighbour, whoever is doing the shop.

- One list at a time. A visitor cannot ask for another one and cannot discover that
  another one exists.
- Read-only. Ticking needs an account.
- Revocable. Unpublish it and the link stops working.
- You choose whether quantities and dates appear, and whether contributors are named —
  names are off by default, because a public page is about what needs buying rather than
  about who lives there.

Publishing is an instance setting: see [Instance settings](/docs/admin/instance).

## Who did what

Every item says who added it, and every tick says who ticked it. When a change came
through an access token the row says so too — *Anna · via Kitchen tablet* — because a
token is somebody's access narrowed, never an identity of its own.
